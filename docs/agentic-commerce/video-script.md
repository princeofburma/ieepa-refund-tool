# Video Script & Storyboard — "Legibility Is the New Shelf Space"
### Explainer on agent-to-agent commerce | Target run time: ~3:30 | Audience: mixed / thought-leadership

**How to read this:** Two columns of intent per scene — **[VISUAL]** describes what's on screen (so a
designer or an AI video tool like HeyGen/Canva can build it), **[VO]** is the voiceover. Timecodes are
approximate. Tone: confident, plainspoken, a little provocative. Think "the calm narrator who's seen the
future and is slightly surprised you haven't."

---

### SCENE 1 — Cold open: the deal you didn't see (0:00–0:20)
**[VISUAL]** Black screen. A single line of text types out: *"Right now, two machines are closing a deal
on your behalf."* Cut to a split screen: a tired executive asleep on a red-eye flight (left) and a quiet
office at night (right). Between them, two glowing nodes pulse and exchange packets of light.

**[VO]** "Somewhere over the Atlantic, an executive is asleep. On the ground, a car service is closed
for the night. Neither will lift a finger. And yet — by the time she lands, a deal will be done. Not by
people. By their agents."

---

### SCENE 2 — The shallow end (0:20–0:45)
**[VISUAL]** Bright, familiar kitchen. A phone screen: *"Milk running low — reordered. $18.40."* A single
node reaches into a grocery shelf. Clean, almost mundane.

**[VO]** "You've probably seen the small version of this. Your assistant reorders the milk. Books the
usual. Refills the printer ink. Useful — but this is the shallow end. One agent, pressing buttons on a
website a little faster than you would. The real shift is deeper. It's when *your* agent starts talking
to *someone else's* agent."

---

### SCENE 3 — The handshake (the limo) (0:45–1:25)
**[VISUAL]** The two nodes from the cold open, now center stage. A clean handshake animation: structured
data flows between them — *pickup, flight BA287, executive SUV, $140, on-time guarantee.* The plane lands;
a black SUV is already waiting. Then pull back: behind the winning car service, three other companies sit
greyed-out, phones ringing into the void, slowly fading.

**[VO]** "Her flight lands three hours late. Her scheduling agent reaches out — not to a website, not to
a dispatcher — to the *agents* of trusted car services. They exchange the trip, the timing, the price.
They agree. She and the driver never spoke. She just sees: 'Car booked. Meeting you at arrivals.'
 ... But notice who *wasn't* in the room. The three best car services in town — invisible. Because their
agents don't exist. A good-enough competitor with an agent just won a fare from the best, who never even
knew the request happened."

---

### SCENE 4 — The turn (1:25–1:45)
**[VISUAL]** Text slams onto screen, big: **"If your business can't be read by an agent — it doesn't
exist."** Behind it, a city of storefronts; the ones without a glowing node go dark one by one.

**[VO]** "That's the turn. In this world, having an agent stops being clever and starts being the cost of
admission. The SEO era was about being *found* by people. This era is about being *transactable* by
machines. Legibility is the new shelf space."

---

### SCENE 5 — Going deeper: the spec (furniture) (1:45–2:20)
**[VISUAL]** A structured spec sheet slides in on the left — *40 ergonomic chairs, 130kg capacity,
10-year warranty, GREENGUARD certified, under $650, deliver to Austin by August.* On the right, supplier
agents light up field-by-field: green check, green check… one flashes red on "warranty" and drops out. A
leaderboard sorts the rest automatically.

**[VO]** "Now go deeper — into how companies actually buy. A business needs forty ergonomic chairs. Its
agent doesn't browse a catalog. It states *exactly* what it needs — capacity, warranty, certifications,
budget, delivery date — and it will only deal with a supplier whose agent can answer, point for point.
Selling stops being a pitch. It becomes proof. The supplier who structured their data and put an agent
on the line gets the order. The one with the beautiful brochure and the slow rep? Never makes the
shortlist."

---

### SCENE 6 — Negotiation and the supply web (2:20–2:55)
**[VISUAL]** Quick montage. First: a negotiation graph — dots (offers, counter-offers) converging on a
price/lead-time plane, with a glowing "policy guardrail" box keeping the agent in bounds, then a single
"needs human sign-off" flag popping up. Then: zoom out to an orchestrator node fanning into dozens of
supplier negotiations, a project timeline assembling itself.

**[VO]** "Deeper still, agents negotiate — trading price against lead time against volume, inside limits
their humans set, escalating the big calls back to a person. And at the top, one instruction —
'stand up a new office by October' — fans out into dozens of deals, coordinated, sequenced, and
reconciled, agent to agent. A single intent becomes an entire supply chain that runs itself."

---

### SCENE 7 — The readiness ladder (2:55–3:15)
**[VISUAL]** A clean 6-rung ladder animates upward: **0 Invisible → 1 Discoverable → 2 Queryable →
3 Transactable → 4 Negotiable → 5 Autonomous partner.** A marker labeled "most businesses today" sits
at the very bottom, blinking at Level 0.

**[VO]** "So here's the honest question for any business: where are you on this ladder? Invisible?
Discoverable? Can an agent actually *transact* with you today? Most companies are sitting at zero — and
they don't know the buyers are already machines."

---

### SCENE 8 — Close & call to action (3:15–3:30)
**[VISUAL]** Back to the two glowing nodes, now one of *many* across a live map of a city, all
transacting. Final card: **"Make your business legible to the machines that are about to do the buying."**
plus logo / URL.

**[VO]** "The plumbing already exists. The only thing missing is businesses making themselves legible.
The ones who move first will quietly absorb the deal flow before anyone else notices the shift. The
question isn't whether the agents are coming. They're already here. The question is whether yours can
answer when they call."

---

## Production notes

- **Length variants:** This is the ~3:30 flagship. For social, cut a **0:45 teaser** from Scenes 1, 3,
  and 4 (cold open → handshake → "doesn't exist" turn). That sequence is the hook.
- **Recurring visual motif:** the glowing node = an agent. Grey/dark = no agent (invisible). Keep this
  consistent so the "fading competitor" beat lands without explanation.
- **Music:** restrained, building synth; a beat-drop on the Scene 4 turn ("doesn't exist").
- **Accessibility:** burn in captions; the structured-data callouts double as on-screen text.
- **If generated via HeyGen:** use a single calm narrator avatar for continuity, with the scene
  descriptions above driving b-roll/graphics. If via Canva/Gamma: build Scenes as slides and export to
  video, using the [VISUAL] notes as layout briefs.
